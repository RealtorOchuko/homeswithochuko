Living in San Francisco

Why people choose San Francisco.

  • Walkable everything

    Most neighborhoods have a commercial corridor within a few blocks, with cafés, groceries and dining you can reach on foot.

  • Transit-rich

    BART, Muni Metro, Caltrain and ferries connect the city to the Peninsula, East Bay and Marin.

  • Microclimates matter

    The Mission and Noe Valley are often sunny while the Sunset sits in fog. I'll help you decide which matters to you.

  • Architecture with history

    Painted Ladies, Edwardian flats and mid-century homes. Older homes reward a careful look at inspections and disclosures.

For investors

Running the numbers in San Francisco.

General notes, not legal or tax advice. Rules change, so we'll confirm specifics for each property.

Rent control

Most rental units in buildings built before June 1979 fall under the SF Rent Ordinance. It shapes your rent growth and exit options.

Transfer tax

SF's transfer tax rises steeply at higher price tiers. Build it into your hold-period math from day one.

TICs & 2-4 units

Tenancy-in-common and small multi-unit buildings can be an entry point, but financing and governance need a careful look.

Common questions.

What do homes cost in San Francisco?

It varies widely by neighborhood and type. Condos commonly start around $800K, single-family homes in popular neighborhoods often run $1.5M to $3M+, and luxury homes go far higher. Ask me for current comps in the neighborhoods you're considering.

Do San Francisco homes sell over asking?

Many single-family homes are priced to draw multiple offers and sell above list, while condos often trade closer to asking. I'll show you recent sale-to-list ratios for the exact area.

What's a TIC?

A tenancy-in-common is shared ownership of a building, with each owner having the right to occupy a specific unit. TICs often cost less than condos but need specialized financing.
