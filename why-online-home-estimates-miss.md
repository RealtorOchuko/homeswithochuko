Almost every homeowner I meet has checked an online estimate. It's a reasonable place to start. But in the Bay Area, where two homes a few blocks apart can sell very differently, an automated number is a rough guess at best.

Here's why, and what I look at instead when I prepare a valuation.

## What the algorithm can't see

Automated estimates are built from public records: square footage, bedroom count, lot size, tax history and nearby sales. They don't know:

- **What's been done inside.** A remodeled kitchen, new windows, a primary suite addition or a finished basement often never shows up in public records.
- **Condition.** Two 1,800-square-foot homes can be worlds apart if one needs a new roof and foundation work and the other is move-in ready.
- **The block.** A quiet cul-de-sac, a view, proximity to a park or a busy street all change what buyers will pay. Algorithms tend to average across a wider area.
- **Unpermitted space.** A garage conversion might add living space, but buyers and lenders value permitted and unpermitted space differently.
- **What's for sale right now.** Your price depends on the homes buyers are comparing yours against this month, not only on what sold last quarter.

## What goes into a real valuation

When I prepare a comparative market analysis (CMA), I start with the closest true comparables: similar homes, nearby, sold recently. Then I adjust for the differences.

1. **Recent sales.** The best two to five comparables, adjusted for size, condition, lot, parking and upgrades.
2. **Active and pending listings.** Your competition. Pending sales are especially useful because they show where the market is heading before those sales close.
3. **Days on market and price changes.** Whether homes near you are selling quickly over asking or sitting and taking reductions tells me a lot about pricing strategy.
4. **The home itself.** This is where my background in residential construction helps. I look at systems, structure and finishes, and I can tell you which repairs buyers will discount heavily and which they'll overlook.
